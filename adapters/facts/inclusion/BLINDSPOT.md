# What `facts/inclusion` cannot see

It says what a shelf admission looks like and what the rule behind it is. It does not decide whether
a work is admitted, and it cannot see any of the following.

## It is one of several routes and knows nothing of the others

A work reaches the database because a publisher's imprint says yuri, because a platform tagged it,
because a magazine ran it, or because a shop shelved it. This owns the last of those. The ranking of
the four is `adapters/classify/credence.py`, the vocabulary of what counts as a platform label is
`facts/marketing`, and DEFINITIONS §6 is the rule all of them serve.

## The shelf a shop calls yuri is a shop's decision

`tag 14 (百合)` is where BOOK☆WALKER files these books today. A shop that renumbers its tags, or
merges two, changes what the record means without changing a word of it. The shelf is recorded
rather than interpreted precisely so that this stays visible, and nothing here notices when it moves.

## An absent shelf is not an absent stock

A shop that carries a work and has not shelved it under yuri produces no admission. The corpus
therefore under-counts wherever a platform hides the category or files a work under a sub-genre, and
the count of admissions is a count of what one shelf listed on one day.

## The rule sentence is a citation and not an enforcement

Every record says a shelf is never a `marketing_label`. Nothing in this module stops a route writing
a `marketing_label` alongside a shelf admission, and the check for that is
`records carrying a publisher-side yuri label whose imprint holds no term saying so`.

## Nothing here can tell a rebuttal from a silence

§2 makes a shelf admission presumptive and rebuttable. A work rebutted and a work nobody has looked
at both hold the same admission record, because a rebuttal is written elsewhere.
