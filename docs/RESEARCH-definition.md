# Research — how 百合 is defined in Japanese sources

Retrieved 2026-08-03. Paraphrase throughout, per [DEFINITIONS](DEFINITIONS.md) §5: publisher and
editorial text is copyrighted, so what is recorded here is our own summary with the source named.
Short attributed phrases only.

**Why this exists.** `DEFINITIONS` §3 defines `content_tier` around romance — canonical, implied,
class-s, incidental — and that is tighter than Japanese usage. This gathers what Japanese sources
actually say, so the definition can be argued from the record rather than from assumption.

Most of this is **Tier C** (reference, fan and editorial). REQUIREMENTS explicitly permits Tier C

## 1. The native vocabulary is 広義 / 狭義, and we should use it

Japanese discussion does not frame this as a ladder of romantic explicitness. It frames it as a
**broad sense and a narrow sense**:

- **広義（broad）** — intimate relationships between women, romantic *or not*
- **狭義（narrow）** — romantic relationships between women; used more or less interchangeably with
  **GL（ガールズラブ）**

The distinction from GL is the sharpest line anyone draws, and it is not about explicitness — it is
about **whether romance is settled**. GL presupposes a couple; 百合 centres the relationship and its
emotional texture, and frequently leaves the romantic question open. A work can be unambiguously
百合 and unambiguously not GL.

Sources: [Wikipedia 百合(ジャンル)](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/百合_(ジャンル)),
[ユリテラシーと現代](https://yuriteracy.hatenablog.com/entry/2018/09/14/165016),
[百合妖精](https://lilium-fairy.com/yuri-anime-manga001/) — retrieved 2026-08-03.

**The debate has a direction.** Several accounts describe the same movement: the older formulation
was roughly "finding female homosexuality appealing", and the definition widened through the
定義論争 to "finding *strong relationships between women* appealing". The broadening is the settled
outcome, not an open question — which is the substance of the correction to §3.

## 2. Editors of コミック百合姫 — the industry position

[Hayakawa Books](https://www.hayakawabooks.com/n/n377845272272), interview by 柴田勝家 with the
founding and current editors-in-chief. Retrieved 2026-08-03. Tier B in substance — this is the
magazine that defines the commercial category — though published by a third party.

- **中村成太郎** (founding editor) glosses 百合 as female relationships seen from a **third-person
  perspective**.
- **梅澤佳奈子** (current editor) puts it as the relational dynamics particular to women, or one
  woman's feelings toward another.
- **Both decline to fix a boundary**, deferring to the reader's own reading. Umezawa notes that the
  magazine's *own creators* disagree about where the edges are.
- **Non-romantic works run in the magazine** — an everyday-life serial about four girls is given as
  an example. But the editors are clear that emotional weight and relational depth are what
  qualifies it; ordinary friendship narrative does not.
- **The magazine's own position has moved.** Early on (as 百合姉妹, from 2003) it marketed works
  from elsewhere aggressively as 百合 to widen the category. After ゆるゆり's success around 2010 it
  went the other way, returning to romantic work as the core.

**This is the most useful finding for us.** The qualifying test the editors describe is not
*romance* — it is **centrality and depth of the relationship**. That is exactly the outer boundary
proposed for this project, arrived at independently by the people who publish the genre.

## 3. Authors on the definition

**森島明子** — quoted in coverage of 百合の世界入門
([コミックナタリー](https://natalie.mu/comic/news/205927), retrieved 2026-08-03). Her distinction
is between identity and observation: being a lesbian is something a person *is*, alone; 百合 is
constituted by *looking at two people together*. On her account it does not depend on what the
characters believe about themselves — it is a reading made from outside.

**仲谷鳰** (やがて君になる) — interviewed by
[百合ナビ](https://yurinavi.com/2018/09/22/nakataninio-interview/), retrieved 2026-08-03, under a
title meaning roughly "the dead centre of 百合 as I see it". She resists a single definition and
stresses variation between characters: liking a girl may happen despite gender, because of gender,
or simply because of the particular person. She explicitly includes characters with no capacity for
romantic feeling at all, and describes what she values as relationships strong enough to override
reason (理屈をねじ伏せてしまうような強い関係性).

**The two authors agree on the thing that matters here** and disagree with our current §3: neither
makes romance the criterion. Morishima makes it observer-relative; Nakatani makes it about
relational force, and admits the aromantic case outright.

## 4. What this means for `content_tier`

1. **The outer boundary should be relational centrality, not romance.** Editors and authors
   converge on this independently. A work primarily concerned with a close relationship between
   women is 百合 in the broad sense whatever the relationship's nature.
2. **There is no value in our schema for "central, close, not romantic."** That is the gap. A work
   like the everyday-life serial the editors cite has to be miscoded as `strongly-implied`
   (asserting romance the work does not) or `incidental` (denying the centrality it plainly has).
3. **狭義 / GL is a real and separate distinction**, and it is about whether romance is *settled* —
   not how explicit it is. Worth keeping distinct from `canonical-romance`, which is an explicitness
   test.
4. **Observer-relativity supports attestation over adjudication.** If two credible sources read a
   work differently, on Morishima's account they are not both wrong — the reading is where the genre
   lives. Recording who says what is the honest model.
5. **A source's date matters.** 百合姫's own position moved around 2010. An attestation from 2007
   and one from 2024 may disagree because the category moved, not because either is mistaken.
